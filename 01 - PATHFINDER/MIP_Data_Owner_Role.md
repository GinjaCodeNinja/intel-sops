**The short version:** You've been named as a data owner. That means one specific area of your organization's information is yours to keep in good shape — so that Copilot can be trusted to work with it. Ongoing commitment: **~30–60 min per quarter.**

## What a data owner is
A **data owner** is the person accountable for a defined area of organizational information. Not the person who created it, not the IT department — the person who knows whether it's **current, correct, and accessible to the right people.** If something in your area is wrong, stale, or in the wrong hands, you're the one who fixes it — or finds someone who can.

## What this role is NOT
- A full-time job or a new title
- An IT role — you don't need to be technical
- Responsibility for data you don't already know
- Liability for historical decisions you didn't make
- A solo job — NCI supports the process
- Something that needs to be perfect before it starts

## Your four responsibilities
1. **Know what's in your area.** Understand what information your area contains and roughly where it lives — Microsoft 365, a dedicated software system, shared drives, or elsewhere. You don't need a perfect inventory; you need enough familiarity to spot when something is wrong.
2. **Keep it current and accurate.** Stale, outdated, or duplicate content is a problem for Copilot — and for your team. On a regular cadence, review your area for content that should be archived, updated, or removed. A practical starting point: **files not opened in ~4 years** — archive what's no longer relevant, keep what still matters (delete only once a backup is confirmed). NCI provides guidance and tools where your data is in Microsoft 365.
3. **Ensure permissions are correct.** The right people should have access — and the wrong people shouldn't. This matters especially before Copilot is enabled, since **Copilot respects existing permissions. If permissions are wrong, Copilot's answers will be wrong.** Watch for access that's broader than you intend — **old "anyone with the link" sharing links, inherited permissions, and access people get through a security group.** Review who has access at least once per cycle.
4. **Approve your area for Copilot access.** When your area has been reviewed and cleaned up, you confirm it's ready. NCI validates with a technical check. **AI stays off for your area until you sign off** — and every activation is **recorded**, so there's a clear trail of what was approved, by whom, and when. Your sign-off matters: **no owner approval, no Copilot access** for that area. This is governance working as designed.

> **When you sit down to review a workspace,** work through the **Workspace Review Checklist** (`NCA_Workspace_Review_Checklist.md`) with NCI — a short guide that walks you from "what is this and who should see it?" to a clean/archive/exclude decision and sign-off.

## Ongoing maintenance cadence
> *Facilitator note (internal): the specific tools per step depend on where the client's data lives. For data in Microsoft 365, AvePoint provides structured reporting for steps 1–3. Outside M365, review is manual or tool-dependent. Tailor this per client.*

| Frequency | Task | What to look for | How (generic) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quarterly | Content review | Stale/outdated files, duplicate versions, content to archive or delete | Walk your area manually, or use your org's review tools |
| Quarterly | Permissions review | People who no longer need access; missing access for current team; external sharing that shouldn't exist | Review access lists with your manager/IT; use reporting tools where available |
| On change | Staff changes | New members need access; departing staff access removed promptly | Notify your IT contact when staff changes affect your area — don't wait for the quarterly cycle |
| On change | Process changes | If a business process changes, its data may need to move, be renamed, or retired | Flag to NCI if a major process change affects your data area |
| Annual | Full area audit | Is the scope still accurate? New data not yet covered? Right tools and people in place? | Review with NCI at the annual AI governance check-in |

## Approving your area for Copilot access
When your area is ready, approval follows a simple four-step flow. Once approved, Copilot can access your area. This is the core of the ongoing cleanup cycle.

1. **You review & clean up** — content current, permissions correct, structure tidy.
2. **You confirm it's ready** — your sign-off that the area meets the agreed standard.
3. **NCI validates** — technical check using available tools; green light or feedback.
4. **Copilot access enabled** — for this area only, specifically and intentionally.
