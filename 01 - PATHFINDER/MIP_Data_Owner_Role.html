The short version: You've been named as a data owner. That means one specific area of your organization's information is yours to keep in good shape — so that Copilot can be trusted to work with it.

A data owner is the person accountable for a defined area of organizational information. Not the person who created it, not the IT department — the person who knows whether it's current, correct, and accessible to the right people. If something in your area is wrong, stale, or in the wrong hands, you're the one who fixes it — or finds someone who can.

What this role is NOT
  • A full-time job or a new title
  • An IT role — you don't need to be technical
  • Responsibility for data you don't already know
  • Liability for historical decisions you didn't make
  • A solo job — NCI supports the process
  • Something that needs to be perfect before it starts
Your Four Responsibilities
1
Know what's in your area
Understand what information your area contains and roughly where it lives — whether that's in Microsoft 365, a dedicated software system, shared drives, or somewhere else. You don't need a perfect inventory. You need enough familiarity to spot when something is wrong.
2
Keep it current and accurate
Stale, outdated, or duplicate content is a problem for Copilot — and for your team. On a regular cadence, review your area for content that should be archived, updated, or removed. NCI will provide guidance and tools to help where your data is in Microsoft 365.
3
Ensure permissions are correct
The right people should have access to your area — and the wrong people shouldn't. This is especially important before Copilot is enabled, since Copilot respects existing permissions. If permissions are wrong, Copilot's answers will be wrong. Review who has access at least once per cycle.
4
Approve your area for Copilot access
When your area has been reviewed and cleaned up, you confirm it's ready. NCI validates this with a technical check. Your sign-off matters — no owner approval, no Copilot access for that area. This is governance working as designed.
Ongoing Maintenance Cadence

For data in Microsoft 365, AvePoint provides structured reporting to support steps 1–3. For data outside M365, the review process is manual or tool-dependent on the system involved. On change Process changes If a business process changes, the data that supports it may need to move, be renamed, or be retired Flag to NCI if a major process change affects the structure of your data area
Annual Full area audit Is the scope of your area still accurate? Has new data been added that isn't covered? Are the right tools and people in place? Review with NCI as part of annual AI governance check-in
