Stage 2 Session Design

Three structured sessions guiding business owners from AI readiness assessment through to safe, governed Copilot activation — in roughly four weeks.

Stage 2 — AI Readiness Assessment

The idea: Three sessions over roughly four weeks. Session 1 aligns and educates. The assessment sees what's actually there. Session 2 delivers the findings plainly. Session 3 turns those findings into a practial action plan. At the end of Stage 3, the business has safely enabled AI — one data area at a time.
Three sessions. One outcome: safely enabled AI.

Every Stage 2 client follows the same path: Assessment Kickoff → technical review → Report Review → AI Roadmap Planning. Sequential, lean, and designed for SMB executives who have limited time and no prior experience with AI governance. The process builds their knowledge as it goes — so that by Session 3, they are making real decisions with genuine confidence.

How it all fits together — Stage 2 at a glance
1
Session One · Assessment Kickoff
Assessment Kickoff
⏱ 60–75 minutes 📅 Start of Stage 2 👥 Business owner + key executives + NCI AM/SE
Part A Framing the Journey ~15 min

Connect where they've been to where they're going. They've done Stage 1 — they've used Copilot, they liked it. Now the question is: what has to be true before we can do this safely?

  • Introduce the Readiness Level framework before they ever see their number. Normalize Level 1. "Most organizations your size are at Level 1. It's not a grade — it's a starting line."
  • Explain what the assessment covers — security posture, document organization, ownership, existing policies
  • Set expectations for the report — plain-language findings, designed to stand on their own, shareable with a lawyer or compliance advisor
  • Introduce the governance concept briefly — the main finding is usually not a security problem, it's that nobody has made decisions yet about who's responsible for what
Set expectations for what comes next

Let the exec know two more sessions follow the assessment. "Once the technical review is complete — roughly two weeks — we'll meet to walk through the findings together. Then we'll have a working session with you and a few key people to turn those findings into an action plan. Both sessions are straightforward, and nothing gets decided today."

Part B The Three Questions That Drive Everything ~20 min

This is the pre-assessment alignment conversation elevated into a leadership discussion. Frame it clearly:

"Before we start the technical assessment, there are three questions only you can answer. Your answers will shape everything — what we look at, what we prioritize, and what Copilot actually does for your business."
Question 1
What do you actually want Copilot to have access to?
Walk through categories using the Process Map: client files, internal operations, finance, HR, email and calendar, Teams conversations. The goal is getting the executive thinking in terms of intentional access rather than default access — most haven't considered these are different things.
Question 2
What is your source of truth for each of those areas?
This one usually produces a pause. For Copilot to give reliable answers, it needs to know where the real version of something lives. Most executives haven't had to write this down before. The goal is surfacing the question, not collecting the answer today.
Question 3
Does each of those areas have a responsible owner — and do they know it?
The second half of this question is what lands. Most executives can name someone for each area. Far fewer are confident that person knows they're accountable for it. The goal is surfacing the accountability gap — not filling it today. That happens in Session 3.

The goal here is not to gather complete answers today. It's to ensure the executive starts thinking about intentional access, data quality, and ownership — so the report's findings already have a mental home when they arrive.

Part C Vocabulary Bridge — From the Room to the Report ~10 min

The three questions we just worked through have names in the report. This section translates what the exec just said in plain language into the terms they'll see when the findings arrive. The goal is zero surprises — not a governance lesson.

"The report will use four terms for what we just discussed. You'll recognize all of them when they show up."
  • Information Scope — Copilot doesn't have a preference for what it reads; it reads everything it can reach. This maps to what you described in Question 1. The report will identify what's currently in scope — and what you may not have realized was included.
  • Source of Truth — The version problem from Question 2. Where the real version of something lives is often not written down anywhere. The assessment will tell you what we can see inside Microsoft 365, but source of truth is ultimately a business decision, not a tool output. The report will start that conversation — it won't finish it.
  • Data Ownership — The accountability question from Question 3. Every data area needs a named person responsible for keeping it current and accurate. The report will show where owners are missing or unclear.
  • Shadow AI and the AI Acceptable Use Policy — One concept we haven't covered yet: your staff are probably already using AI tools outside of Copilot. That's not a discipline problem — it's a signal that your team wants to work this way. One of the things we deliver alongside the report is an example AI Acceptable Use Policy — a starting point for the conversation about what's approved, what isn't, and what the rules are. Note: the example is exactly that — an example. Any policy your organization actually adopts should be developed with your own legal counsel.
Part D Your AI Readiness Decisions — The Reflection Guide ~10 min

Don't call it homework. Hand over the Reflection Guide with clear framing:

"These aren't questions we need answered to start the assessment — we're already starting. These are questions worth sitting with so that when the report arrives, you're already thinking in the right direction. Grab a coffee, 15 minutes."
Section What It Asks Format
What wouldn't you want Copilot to read? Easier to define the fence than the field — this is the entry question Open reflection
Where does time disappear in your week? Replaces "where could AI help?" — answerable without AI knowledge Open reflection
Who do your people go to for the real answer? Natural data owner identification — revisits Q3 in private; honest answers differ from in-room answers Names / roles
Potential data owners by area Rough names against content areas — blank rows are just as useful Simple table
What's your honest concern about AI? Surfaces the real worry before Session 3 — not the polished answer Open reflection
What does success look like in 6 months? Personal north star — we refer back to this in Session 3 Open reflection
Facilitator note — intentional overlap

Questions 3 and 4 of the Reflection Guide revisit ground covered in Part B Question 3. That's deliberate. What an executive writes down alone over coffee is often more honest than what they said in the room. Don't skip them.

Also hand over the Process Map

The Core Process Map was completed together in the room during Part B. Leave a copy with the exec — it's a shared reference. NCI keeps a copy too. It comes back in Session 3 when we confirm scope and assign owners area by area.

2
Session Two · Report Review
Report Review
⏱ 30–45 minutes 📅 3–5 days after report delivery 👥 Business owner + NCI AM/SE
This session has one job: make sure the exec understands what the report found and feels settled about what comes next. No decisions are made here. No governance plan is built here. Nothing is sold here. Session 3 does all of that — this session just ensures the exec arrives there informed.
Part A Initial Reactions First ~10 min

Don't open with the report. Open with a check-in. The exec has had a few days alone with the findings — give them space to say what landed before you present anything.

"You've had a couple of days with the report. Before we walk through it together — what's your initial read?"

This surfaces real reactions and tells the facilitator where to spend energy. Common responses:

  • Anxious — "This feels like a lot." → Normalize the findings. Remind them this is what organizations their size look like before anyone has made deliberate decisions about AI. It's the starting line.
  • Curious — "I had no idea Copilot could see all that." → Good. The vocabulary from Session 1 Part C did its job. Follow the thread.
  • Settled — "Most of this matches what I expected." → Confirm their read, then move to Part B. Don't add drama.
  • Dismissive — "IT handles all this." → Gently redirect. Data ownership and access decisions are leadership decisions, not IT decisions. Session 3 makes that concrete.
Part B The Findings Walk ~20 min

Walk through each section of the report together using plain language. The exec already has the vocabulary from Session 1 Part C — use it without re-explaining it. Anchor findings to the process map from Session 1 where you can.

  • Security posture — What the foundation looks like. This is usually the good news — frame it as "something to build on."
  • Information scope — What Copilot currently has access to. Often broader than the exec expected. Connect this back to their Q1 answer from Session 1.
  • Source of truth gaps — Where multiple versions of things exist or where the authoritative copy is unclear. This is expected at this stage.
  • Ownership gaps — Where named accountable owners are missing. This is the most common finding. It's not negligence — it's the natural result of using M365 practically without anticipating AI.
Keep this human

If a finding lands hard or produces a strong reaction — slow down. A brief "does that make sense?" or "what does that bring up for you?" is more useful than moving to the next slide. The goal is that they leave feeling informed, not overwhelmed.

Close every finding with: "This is what your environment looks like today — not a verdict. Session 3 is where we decide what to do about it."

Closing Confirm Session 3 and Close ~5 min

End simply. Don't introduce new concepts. Don't start the governance conversation.

"Nothing to decide today — you now know what the assessment found. Session 3 is where we turn this into a plan. You'll want to bring a few of the people we talked about in Session 1 — the ones most likely to become data owners. Does the picture make sense? Any questions before we close?"

Confirm Session 3 is scheduled before you leave the room. If it isn't, schedule it now. The momentum from this session is the fuel for that one.

Session 3 — AI Roadmap Planning · 3–5 days after Session 2
3
Session Three · AI Roadmap Planning
AI Roadmap Planning
⏱ 60–90 minutes 📅 3–5 days after Session 2 👥 Business owner + likely data owners + NCI AM/SE
This is a working session. Decisions get made, owners get named, and the Stage 3 queue gets built. The exec should bring the people most likely to become data owners — identified in Session 1 Question 3 and the Reflection Guide. NCI should arrive with a one-page scope summary prepared from the Session 1 process map and report findings, so the group isn't starting from blank.
Part A Confirm Scope — What Goes In, What Stays Out ~15 min

Revisit the Process Map from Session 1 area by area. For each area, confirm two things:

Question Yes answer No / Not yet answer
In scope for Copilot? Enters the Stage 3 work queue Noted — revisit in a later cycle or exclude permanently
Data in Microsoft 365? AvePoint can assess it · Stage 3 can address it Flagged for separate solution discussion — migration, MCP server, or other approach
The non-M365 conversation

If important data lives outside Microsoft 365 — in a line-of-business application, a file server, or a third-party system — that data is currently invisible to Copilot. Getting Copilot access to it requires a separate solution. Note these areas without derailing the session; they become follow-up conversations or proposals after Stage 3 is underway.

Output of Part A: A confirmed scope list — the areas entering Stage 3 — and a flag list of non-M365 areas for follow-up.

Part B Name the Data Owners ~20 min

For each in-scope area from Part A, name one person accountable for it. Work through the list together — drawing on the Reflection Guide Q3/Q4 answers the exec already did privately.

"For each area — who owns it, and does that person know they're being named today?"
  • One owner per area. Not a team, not a department — one named person. They don't need to do the work alone, but they need to be the one accountable for it.
  • They need to know. If the right person isn't in the room, the exec commits to telling them before Stage 3 starts. An owner who doesn't know they're an owner is not an owner.
  • What owning an area means in practice: keeping content current, ensuring permissions are accurate, checking AvePoint reports on a schedule, and approving their area for Copilot access when it's clean. A data owner one-pager will be provided at closeout.
  • If no one can be named for an area — that area does not get enabled. No owner, no access. This is the governance model working as designed.

Output of Part B: A data owner registry — one named person per area — filled in and confirmed in the room.

Part C Prioritize the Stage 3 Queue ~15 min

The areas from Part A can't all be addressed at once. Sequence them using three criteria:

  • Value — Where would Copilot provide the most immediate, visible benefit? Use the exec's Reflection Guide answers and Session 1 Q1 intent as the guide.
  • Readiness — Where is the data cleanest and most organized today? Starting with a cleaner area builds confidence and momentum. The report findings will show this clearly.
  • Owner motivation — Where is the named owner most engaged and capable of moving quickly? An enthusiastic owner makes Area 1 succeed; a reluctant one stalls it.

Output of Part C: A sequenced priority list — Area 1 goes into Stage 3 first, others follow in agreed order. This becomes the Stage 3 work plan. It does not need to be a formal document today — a clear verbal agreement confirmed in a follow-up email is sufficient.

Part D What Happens Next — The Rinse & Repeat Cycle + AUP ~10–15 min

Close the session by making Stage 3 concrete — and by handing over the example AI AUP with the right framing.

The rinse and repeat cycle, explained simply:

  • Step 1 — Owner reviews their area. Using AvePoint and their own knowledge: is this content current? Are permissions correct? Is anything stale or in the wrong place?
  • Step 2 — Owner cleans up. Archive stale content, correct permissions, tidy structure. NCI can provide guidance but the owner does the work — this is their data.
  • Step 3 — NCI validates with AvePoint. NCI runs an AvePoint check to confirm the area meets the agreed standard. If it passes — green light.
  • Step 4 — Copilot access enabled for that area. Specifically and intentionally — not everything, just this area.
  • Step 5 — Move to next area in the priority queue and repeat.

Before handing over the AI AUP example, ask one question:

"Before we hand this over — what's most important to you about how AI is used in your business? What would be completely unacceptable?"

This surfaces the exec's non-negotiables before they read the example — so the policy feels like it reflects their values, not just a template from NCI. Note their answer. It should show up in the final policy they adopt.

Important — Example Only: The AI Acceptable Use Policy document provided is an example to help start your thinking. It is not a draft policy, not a template, and is not legal advice. Any AI Acceptable Use Policy your organization actually adopts should be developed with your own legal counsel, who can ensure it reflects your specific situation, jurisdiction, and obligations.

Deliverables handed over at close of Session 3:

📄 Data Owner Registry (filled in today) 📄 Stage 3 Priority Order (Area 1 → Area 2 → …) 📄 Example AI Acceptable Use Policy 📄 Data Owner Role One-Pager
Close by connecting back to their success definition

Refer back to the exec's Reflection Guide Q6 answer — what they said success looks like in 6 months. "You told us [their answer]. That's exactly what this is building toward. Area 1 starts in Stage 3 — and each area we enable gets you closer to that." End on forward momentum, not on instructions.

Why This Process Works for SMBs

  • Three sessions in four weeks — no extended engagement required
  • Executives start from zero — the process teaches as it progresses
  • Governance emerges from the exec's own answers — not imposed from outside
  • One data area at a time — never governing "everything" at once
  • Copilot access is earned by each area — clear criteria, no ambiguity
  • Data owners are named in the room, not assigned by memo
  • The AI AUP is a conversation before it's a document
  • The plan connects directly to what the exec said success looks like
