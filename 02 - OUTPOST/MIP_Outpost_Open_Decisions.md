# Outpost — Open Decisions Register
## AI Discovery Journey · Outpost Stage

**Status:** Internal Working Draft v0.1 — *decisions are the weekly group's to make*
**Purpose:** The running list of *probable decisions* we need to make before Outpost delivery is fully nailed down. Each carries why it matters and a few potential options — **these are starting points, not answers.** The weekly coordination group works this list and records the calls in the "Decided" section.
**How to use:** Pull items into the weekly agenda. When the group decides one, move it to **Decided** and update the Outpost Delivery SOP (`NCA_Outpost_Delivery_SOP.md`), which has matching `⟨DECISION PENDING⟩` flags.
**No owners are pre-assigned** — the group decides who takes what.

## A. Lead handoff & entry into the program

**A1 · Do account managers discuss the AI program, or just flag warm leads?**
- Why it matters: sets what every AM needs to know and how consistent the client message is.
- Options: (a) AMs give a light program overview + hand off; (b) AMs only flag interest and hand off cold; (c) AMs are trained to a scripted talk-track.
- Status: Open

**A2 · Who is the "somebody" a warm lead goes to?**
- Why it matters: without a clear intake point, leads get dropped or double-handled.
- Options: (a) single named intake (one AI Expert or a coordinator); (b) round-robin between AI Experts; (c) a sales lead triages then assigns.
- Status: Open

**A3 · What qualifies a lead as "warm enough," and what does the AM say vs. not say?**
- Why it matters: protects against over-promising and against wasted handoffs.
- Options: (a) simple interest checklist; (b) a couple of qualifying questions AMs ask; (c) no filter — hand off all interest.
- Status: Open

**A4 · Who owns the Outpost upsell conversation for an existing Pathfinder client?**
- Why it matters: this is a relationship + timing call; wrong owner = awkward or missed.
- Options: (a) AM (owns the relationship); (b) a sales lead; (c) the AI Expert already working the account.
- Status: Open

## B. Outpost entry criteria & trigger

**B1 · "Outpost is earned, not sold" — who decides a Pathfinder client is ready, and on what signals?**
- Why it matters: keeps the promise real and gives AMs an objective trigger.
- Signals to consider: N workspaces signed off, active Copilot use, exec sponsor engaged, a working group exists.
- Options: (a) AI Expert calls it from the tracker; (b) a simple readiness checklist anyone can score; (c) group review.
- Status: Open

**B2 · Can a client buy into Outpost directly (skip/compress Pathfinder)?**
- Why it matters: some clients (already using Claude) will want to start high — this is the catch-up scenario.
- Options: (a) yes, with a mandatory Foundation Catch-Up Backlog attached; (b) no, Pathfinder basics first; (c) case-by-case with a governance minimum.
- Lean: (a) — matches the framework's "start anywhere, catch up, never skip."
- Status: Open

**B3 · What's the commercial trigger — when is the MSA addendum signed, and by whom?**
- Why it matters: no delivery should start without scope + terms agreed.
- Options: (a) signed before kickoff; (b) signed at kickoff; (c) verbal start, paper follows (not recommended).
- Status: Open

## C. Kickoff & where we start

**C1 · Is there an Outpost kickoff session before the monthly cadence begins?**
- Why it matters: sets expectations, prevents a vague "so… what now?" first month.
- Options: (a) dedicated kickoff (overview + expectations + stand up working group + pick first focus); (b) fold kickoff into Session 1; (c) no kickoff.
- Lean: (a) — a short kickoff pays for itself in momentum.
- Status: Open

**C2 · Who attends kickoff?**
- Why it matters: the right room = buy-in; the wrong room = stalls.
- Options: exec sponsor, working-group champions, AI Expert, AM — decide which are required vs. optional.
- Status: Open

**C3 · Where does Session 1 actually start?**
- Why it matters: the most-asked question from the AI Experts; needs a default.
- Options: (a) audit current Copilot usage + set fluency baseline; (b) run a Workflow Deconstruction on a real workflow; (c) stand up the working group + pull-menu; (d) reuse the Executive Sessions "two questions" as the on-ramp.
- Lean: a blend — baseline fluency (a) + stand up the group (c), with the "two questions" (d) as framing.
- Status: Open

**C4 · Do we define a default first-90-days arc, or keep it fully client-led?**
- Why it matters: a template makes Outpost repeatable and trainable; too rigid kills the "discover what lands" spirit.
- Options: (a) default arc that's easy to deviate from; (b) fully client-led each time; (c) two or three named "starting paths" by client type.
- Lean: (a) — a soft default, per the "structure available if they want it" principle.
- Status: Open

## D. Session mechanics & cadence

**D1 · Who books the meetings and owns the recurring invite?**
- Why it matters: unbooked sessions = the service quietly dies.
- Options: (a) AI Expert books/owns; (b) an admin/coordinator; (c) client books against the AI Expert's availability.
- Status: Open

**D2 · Do we adopt the existing 4-beat session format as the standard agenda?**
- Why it matters: consistency makes Outpost sellable and scalable.
- Options: (a) yes, adopt `NCA_Fractional_Expert_Session_Format.md` as-is; (b) adopt with tweaks.
- Lean: (a).
- Status: Open

**D3 · Accelerated cadence — if a client wants bi-weekly instead of monthly?**
- Why it matters: real client demand; the AI Consulting Pricing Model already handles this — biweekly/weekly meeting frequency is a direct input to the formula, so pricing adjusts automatically rather than needing a separate package.
- Status: Open — *confirm this fully replaces the old add-on-hours framing.*

**D4 · More than 1 hr/month — how are add-on hours requested, approved, and booked?**
- Why it matters: avoids scope creep and unbilled time.
- Options: (a) client requests → AI Expert confirms → booked + billed at the pricing model's $200/hr rate; (b) pre-purchased block; (c) monthly cap.
- Status: Open

**D5 · What happens when a client goes quiet between sessions?**
- Why it matters: protects value delivery and renewal.
- Options: (a) defined nudge sequence (e.g., 2 check-ins, then flag to AM); (b) AI Expert discretion; (c) no action.
- Status: Open

## E. Build / agent work scope

**E1 · What agent work is included in the monthly hour vs. a billable build sprint?**
- Why it matters: the single biggest scope-creep risk in Outpost.
- Options: (a) included = advise/design/small config; billable = anything beyond ~X effort; (b) all builds billable; (c) a fixed "one small agent" included.
- Status: Open

**E2 · Who builds — the AI Expert, or a PS/dev resource?**
- Why it matters: capacity and skill fit; affects margins.
- Options: (a) AI Expert does light builds, hands complex to PS; (b) all builds to PS at Preferred AI PS rate; (c) AI Expert does all.
- Status: Open

**E3 · Consult-construct boundary — what do we explicitly NOT do?**
- Why it matters: protects NCI and sets client expectations (no outcome guarantees).
- Draft "we don't": make business decisions, own operations, guarantee productivity/accuracy/compliance outcomes, approve AI outputs on the client's behalf.
- Status: Open

**E4 · A build needs data that isn't governed yet — how do we route it?**
- Why it matters: enforces the gate without killing momentum.
- Options: (a) fast-track that workspace through the Rail B cycle; (b) sandbox on dummy data meanwhile; (c) pick a different, already-governed target.
- Lean: (b)+(a) in parallel.
- Status: Open

- Status: Open

## I. Onboarding & the working group

**I1 · Who runs onboarding — AI Expert, AM, or a coordinator?**
- Why it matters: sets who owns getting a client from signed to delivery-ready.
- Options: (a) AI Expert; (b) AM; (c) a program coordinator; (d) split — AM handles commercial, AI Expert handles setup.
- Status: Open

**I2 · Onboarding timeline / SLA — how fast from signed to first session?**
- Why it matters: momentum; the excitement fades if setup drags.
- Options: (a) within ~2 weeks; (b) within 30 days; (c) no set target.
- Lean: (a).
- Status: Open

**I3 · What does NCI need to provision/access, and who supplies it?**
- Why it matters: avoids a setup stalled waiting on access.
- Draft list: Copilot licensing confirmed · tenant/workspace access scoped · tools · a named client admin contact.
- Status: Open

**I4 · Working group composition & size — who's on it, how many, which departments?**
- Why it matters: too big = slow; too narrow = misses uses. The testers are the domain experts who find the value.
- Options: sponsor + 1–2 champions + rotating testers across departments; pick a target size.
- Cost note: **every member needs a full Copilot license**, and group size is also a direct input to the AI Consulting Pricing Model's consulting-hours term — factor both into the target size.
- Status: Open

**I5 · Do we need a client-facing "AI Working Group" deliverable (guide / one-pager)?**
- Why it matters: gives the group a shared picture of what it is, the roles, and the cadence — so it runs between our sessions.
- Note: a `Working Group operating guide` already exists as an internal reference (framework §12) — this decides whether to make a client-facing version.
- Options: (a) a client-facing one-pager; (b) fold it into the kickoff deck; (c) internal-only, nothing client-facing.
- Status: Open

**I6 · Do we need a working-group training lesson (Stage-1 style)?**
- Why it matters: a short lesson on running experiments / using the pull-menu could make the group self-sufficient faster.
- Options: (a) a short dedicated lesson; (b) cover it live in kickoff/Session 1; (c) rely on the experiment cards' self-serve design.
- Status: Open

**I7 · Is Kickoff the last step of onboarding or the first step of delivery?**
- Why it matters: keeps the SOP boundary clean (currently drafted as the last onboarding step).
- Options: (a) last step of onboarding (current draft); (b) first step of delivery.
- Lean: (a).
- Status: Open
